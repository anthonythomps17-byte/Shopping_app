# Belgian Frites with Beef Stew
Serves: 4
Time: 3 hr
Tags: dinner, street food, comfort food, slow cook

Stoofvlees, the rich Flemish beef and beer stew that Belgian chip shops ladle over a cone of frites. Slow-cooked until meltingly tender, with a hint of mustard and spice.

## Ingredients
- 1kg braising steak, cut into 4cm chunks
- 2 tbsp plain flour
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil
- 30g butter
- 2 onions, sliced
- 500ml brown ale
- 300ml beef stock
- 2 bay leaves
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 tbsp soft brown sugar
- 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
- 2 slices bread
- 2 tbsp Dijon mustard
- Salt and black pepper, to taste

### For the frites
- 1.2kg potatoes (floury, like Maris Piper), cut into chips
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil

### To serve
- 4 tbsp mayonnaise

## Method
1. Toss the beef in the flour with plenty of salt and pepper. Heat the oil in a large casserole over a high heat and brown the beef in batches, then lift out.
2. Lower the heat, add the butter and onions and cook for 10 minutes until soft and golden.
3. Return the beef, pour in the ale and stock, and add the bay, thyme, sugar and vinegar. Spread the bread with the mustard and lay it on top, mustard-side down. Bring to a simmer.
4. Cover and cook gently for 2½ hours, stirring now and then (the bread melts in and thickens the sauce), until the beef falls apart. Take the lid off for the last 30 minutes if it needs thickening.
5. For the frites, heat the oven to 220°C (200°C fan), gas 7. Toss the chips with the oil and salt, spread out on a large tray and roast for 40 minutes, turning halfway, until crisp.
6. Pile the frites into bowls, ladle over the stew and serve with a dollop of mayonnaise, the Belgian way.

## Notes
- Some Belgian cooks add a couple of crumbled Biscoff biscuits instead of the sugar for a gingery sweetness.
- The stew is even better the next day, and freezes well.
